## SECTION 2 — WHY NORCO DOGS ARE A DIFFERENT PROJECT

**H2**
## Norco dogs have different problems

Horsetown USA isn't a marketing slogan the city bought — it's written into how the place is built. Bridle trails instead of sidewalks. Hitching posts outside businesses. Half-acre and larger lots with animal keeping baked into the zoning. There are neighborhoods where you'll pass more horses than cars on a Saturday morning.

That produces a specific kind of dog problem, and it isn't the one a training franchise in a shopping center is set up for.

Your dog probably has more space than most dogs in Southern California, and more freedom, and less structure — because when there's an acre of room, nobody bothers with a leash. Then one of these happens:

- The dog gets out. Three-rail fencing keeps a horse in. It does not keep a shepherd mix in for ten minutes.
- The dog decides horses are interesting. Chasing, heeling at hocks, barking at the trailer. This is dangerous for everyone involved, including the dog.
- The dog has no recall. It never needed one until the day it did.
- The dog is fine on the property and unmanageable off it — reactive on the trail, impossible at the feed store, loses its mind at the vet.
- Two or three dogs live on the property and function as a unit, which means nobody has a relationship with any of them individually.

None of that is a bad dog. It's a dog that was never given a job or a boundary, on land generous enough to hide the problem until it stopped being hideable.

## SECTION 4 — OFF-LEASH AND RECALL

**H2**
## The thing most Norco owners actually want

Recall. Not sit. Recall.

If you've got property, the whole point is that your dog gets to use it. That only works if your dog comes back — the first time, while it's moving, with something more interesting in front of it. A recall that works in the kitchen and fails at the fence line isn't a recall, it's a coincidence.

Our Off-Leash Reliability Program is $4,500 and it's built for exactly this. We train recall and obedience that holds at distance and under real distraction, then proof it in places your dog genuinely wants to leave. The e-collar and every piece of equipment is included in the price, and we teach you to run it properly before your dog goes home. We're direct about that tool because finding out mid-program is how people end up angry.

For dogs living around livestock, we also work on neutrality — the dog learns that the horse, the goat, the chickens, and the neighbor's mule are all furniture. Not prey, not a game, not a thing to bark at.

## SECTION 6 — BEHAVIOR WORK

**H2**
## Aggression and reactivity cases from Norco

We take behavior cases seriously and we evaluate every dog individually before accepting one. That's not a hedge — aggression has different causes and the cause changes the plan. A dog that's terrified of the farrier needs something different than a dog that's never once been told no.

Common in this part of the county: dogs that have run at a horse or a rider on the trail, dogs that guard the property line and mean it, dogs that have gotten into it with the other dog on the same acreage, and dogs that came from a rescue with a history nobody can fully account for.

The Behavior Rehabilitation Program is three weeks at $3,500. We won't promise you an outcome, on this or anything else. We'll tell you honestly what we think we can do and whether we're the right people for it.

## Norco dog training FAQ

**Do you offer dog training in Norco, CA?**
Yes. Norco is about ten minutes from our five-acre facility in Corona, and every service we run is available to Norco clients — Board & Train, private lessons, behavior rehabilitation, off-leash training, overnight boarding, and daycare.

**How much does Board & Train cost for Norco dogs?**
$2,900 for three weeks for dogs six months and older, $3,200 for the six-week puppy program, $3,500 for the three-week Behavior Rehabilitation Program, and $4,500 for the Off-Leash Reliability Program. Pricing is the same regardless of which city you're driving from.

**Can you train my dog to leave the horses alone?**
That's a neutrality problem, and it's trainable. We teach the dog that livestock is scenery — not prey, not a toy, not something to bark at — and then proof it around real distractions rather than in a quiet yard. Bring it up on the consultation so we can factor it into the plan.

**My dog has an acre and still won't come when called. Can you fix that?**
Usually, yes. Space is not the same thing as training, and a lot of dogs on large Norco properties have never had a reason to come back until the day something goes wrong. Reliable recall under distraction is what the $4,500 Off-Leash Reliability Program is built to produce.

**Do you offer dog daycare in Norco?**
We don't operate a facility in Norco — daycare happens at our Corona ranch, about ten minutes away. That's close enough that plenty of Norco dogs come regularly. Daycare is $40 a day, kennel-free, with an assessment required before the first visit.

**Can you board more than one dog from the same household?**
Yes, and the second and third dogs are $10 a night less each. Multi-dog households are common out here. Tell us on the phone whether they should share space or be separated, because we won't assume.
