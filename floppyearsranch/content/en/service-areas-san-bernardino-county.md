## We're not your convenient option. Here's when we're the right one.

Let's be straight about the situation. There are trainers along the 210 corridor. If your golden retriever pulls on the leash and jumps on guests, drive fifteen minutes and hire one of them. We'd tell you the same thing on the phone, and it would cost you nothing to hear it.

## SECTION 4 — FOOTHILL DOGS

**H2**
## What the foothills do to a dog

There's a training profile that repeats across this whole corridor, from Rancho Cucamonga to Redlands, and it comes from the geography.

Neighborhoods here end at open space. Above the last row of houses is wildland — canyon, chaparral, and everything living in it. That produces a set of problems you don't get in flat tract suburbs:

- **Dogs that get out and go up.** A dog that slips a gate in Eastvale ends up two streets over. A dog that slips a gate in Alta Loma or north La Verne ends up in a canyon. Recall stops being a convenience.
- **Coyotes.** Routine here, and a bad encounter is one of the more common origins of the reactivity cases we take from this county — often in a dog that was fine the week before.
- **Trail dogs with no manners.** Claremont Hills, Marshall Canyon, North Etiwanda, San Timoteo — these trails are packed on weekend mornings, mostly with other dogs. A leash-reactive dog turns a good hike into an ordeal for everyone on it.
- **Evacuation readiness.** This is fire country, and October is a real month here. A dog that loads into a crate and into a car on command, calmly, at 2am, is a genuine safety asset. We teach crate comfort and car loading in every program because it's basic obedience — but in this corridor it occasionally matters a great deal.

## SECTION 5 — CITY BY CITY

**H2**
## Drive times and local notes

### H3 — Rancho Cucamonga — 30 to 35 minutes
The closest, straight down the 15. Alta Loma and Etiwanda sit against the foothills with the preserve above them; the flats below are dense and dog-heavy. Rancho sits at the near edge of what this page covers — if you're here, the Inland Empire page lists what else is available at that distance.

### H3 — Upland — 35 to 45 minutes
The 15 to the 210 west, or the 71 to the 60 and up. Upland is old for this region — Euclid Avenue's wide center parkway was a bridle path long before it was scenery, and the north-side neighborhoods still have horse property mixed in with everything else. Large-lot dogs with no recall are a familiar call from here.

### H3 — Claremont — 40 to 45 minutes
The 210 west. Claremont is technically Los Angeles County, not San Bernardino — we're listing it here because it's the same foothill corridor and the same drive, and pretending otherwise would just make this page harder to use. Tree-lined streets, the Colleges, the Village, and Claremont Hills Wilderness Park, which is one of the busiest dog trails in the region. Leash manners get tested here weekly.

### H3 — La Verne — about 45 minutes
Also LA County, same corridor, same reasoning. The 210 or the 57. North La Verne has horse properties and the Marshall Canyon trail system, and it produces the acreage-and-no-recall profile as reliably as anywhere we serve.

### H3 — San Bernardino — 45 to 55 minutes
The 15 north to the 210 east, or the 60 east to the 215 north depending on the hour. The city is large, varied, and has a heavy shelter and rescue pipeline, which means a lot of adult dogs land in homes with no reliable account of their first couple of years. Unknown history isn't a barrier for us — we assess the dog in front of us.

### H3 — Redlands — 55 to 65 minutes
The farthest, out the 10 past the 210 junction. Redlands is walkable in a way most of the region isn't — the old neighborhoods around the University, Prospect Park, the groves, the Sunset loop — so dogs here are out in public constantly. If you're coming from this far, be honest with yourself about the second trip. The transfer session is the part that makes the rest of it hold.

## San Bernardino County dog training FAQ

**Do you serve San Bernardino County?**
Yes, for Board & Train and behavior rehabilitation. Our facility is in Corona, which is 30 to 35 minutes from Rancho Cucamonga, 35 to 45 from Upland, 45 to 55 from San Bernardino, and 55 to 65 from Redlands.

**Do you offer boarding or daycare in San Bernardino County?**
No. Daycare and routine overnight boarding are only for dogs within about fifteen minutes of our Corona facility. From this county the drive isn't realistic, and we'd rather tell you that than sell you something you'd stop using. Board & Train and behavior work are drop-off programs, which is why they travel.

**How much does Board & Train cost?**
$2,900 for three weeks for dogs six months and older, $3,200 for the six-week puppy program, $3,500 for the three-week Behavior Rehabilitation Program, and $4,500 for the Off-Leash Reliability Program. Deposits are $500 or $750 depending on the program, and the $50 in-person evaluation is credited toward whatever you book.

**Why drive an hour when there are trainers closer to me?**
For basic obedience, don't. Hire someone nearby and save yourself the freeway. The reasons people make this drive are behavior cases that most trainers won't take, and the space to work them — five acres, with room to work a reactive dog at a distance from its trigger and open ground to proof a recall on.

**Can I do the evaluation without driving out?**
The first thirty-minute phone or video consultation is free and requires no travel. If you want a real assessment before committing to the drive, the $139 virtual consultation runs an hour and covers any behavior. It's not a substitute for hands on the dog, but it will tell you whether the trip is worth making.

**We adopted a dog with no history. Is that a problem?**
No. It's common, and it's most of what we do with rescue cases from this county. We're reading the dog in front of us, not the paperwork. What matters is whether the behavior is fear, frustration, guarding, or learned escalation — each one gets a different plan.

**Do you work with dogs that have run off into open space?**
Yes, and it's a frequent request from the foothill neighborhoods. Reliable recall under distraction is what the $4,500 Off-Leash Reliability Program produces. The e-collar and all equipment are included in the price and we teach you to run it properly before your dog goes home — we'll tell you exactly what we plan to use before you book anything.
