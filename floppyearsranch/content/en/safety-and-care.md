## SECTION 2 — VACCINATION REQUIREMENTS

**H2**
## Vaccination requirements

Every dog on this property — boarding, daycare, Board & Train, or a private lesson — needs the following:

| Vaccine | Required? | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Rabies** | Required | Legally required in California for dogs over four months |
| **DHPP / DA2PP** | Required | Distemper, adenovirus, parvovirus, parainfluenza |
| **Bordetella** | Required | The one that lapses most often — many vets want it every 6–12 months for dogs in group settings |
| **Canine influenza (H3N2/H3N8)** | Recommended | Not required, but worth having for any dog in group environments |

**How to send records.** Bring them at drop-off or have your vet email them ahead. A phone photo of the certificate is fine if the dates are legible.

**We enforce this.** No current records, no admission — including for dogs we've known for years. There's no exception for a dog who "never goes anywhere." A vaccine requirement only protects the group if it's universal.

**Puppies** can start once they've had enough of their series to be safe around other dogs; call and we'll walk through the timing. **If you titer** instead of vaccinating, send the results and we'll talk. Rabies isn't negotiable — that's state law, not our policy.

## SECTION 3 — FEEDING

**H2**
## Feeding

**We feed your dog's food.** Send what they normally eat, in the amount they normally eat, for the length of the stay plus a couple of extra days. Switching a dog's diet at the same moment you switch their whole environment is how you get a week of diarrhea. Pre-bagged meals in labeled zip bags remove all guesswork; otherwise send a scoop and written instructions.

**Tell us the details.** Slow-feeder bowl. Soaked kibble. Toppers mixed a certain way. Eats better if a person stands there. None of it is too fussy to mention.

**Resource guarding.** If your dog guards food from people or other dogs, tell us at drop-off. It's extremely common and we won't judge you for it, but it changes how we feed them. What we can't work around is finding out the hard way.

**Appetite is a health signal.** Skipping a meal on day one is usually settling in. Skipping several is telling us something, and you'll hear about it that day.

## SECTION 4 — MEDICATION

**H2**
## Medication

We give medications. There's no surcharge for it.

**What we need from you:**

- The medication **in its original labeled container** — not a baggie of loose pills
- **Written instructions:** drug, dose, how many times a day, what time, with or without food
- Whatever you normally hide it in, or a note that your dog takes it dry
- A heads-up if your dog is difficult to pill, and how you manage it at home

If a dose is refused, vomited, or spat out, you hear from us. What we won't do is start, stop, or adjust a medication on our own, or give anything that wasn't sent with instructions — including over-the-counter products.

**Behavioral medication.** Plenty of dogs here are on something — trazodone, gabapentin, fluoxetine. That's not a mark against your dog and it doesn't disqualify them from a program. Tell us what they're on and who prescribed it, because it changes how a dog presents and we'd rather not misread a sedated dog as a calm one.

## SECTION 5 — CLEANING AND SANITIZATION

**H2**
## Cleaning and sanitization

**Kennels are cleaned daily and sanitized between dogs.** Bedding is laundered, bowls are washed rather than rinsed, and water bowls and pools are refreshed through the day rather than topped off in the morning.

**The arena uses dog-safe sanitization.** Dogs lie on those surfaces and lick them. Whatever goes on them should be something you're comfortable with a dog contacting — a lower bar than a fair number of facilities clear.

**Waste is picked up continuously**, not on a schedule. It's the cheapest disease control available and it's mostly about whether anybody bothers.

**Isolation.** A dog showing signs of anything contagious comes out of the group immediately and stays out until it's resolved, and you get a call the same day. This is exactly why the Bordetella requirement isn't optional.

## SECTION 6 — HEAT SAFETY

**H2**
## Heat safety in an Inland Empire summer

This is the section that matters most for anyone boarding a dog in Corona between June and September, and the one most facilities skip. Temescal Valley runs triple digits for long stretches, and ground temperature runs far above air temperature — asphalt and standard artificial turf can burn paw pads while a person in shoes notices nothing. Dogs don't sweat, either. They dump heat by panting, and heatstroke can go from "a bit slow" to a medical emergency in well under an hour.

What we actually do:

- **Hard work moves to the ends of the day.** Training and hard play run early and late. Midday is rest, shade, and water. This isn't a preference we take requests on.
- **We check the ground with a hand.** If a surface is uncomfortable to a palm for five seconds, no dog goes on it.
- **Cooled turf in the arena, shade everywhere, climate control indoors, water and pools refreshed all day.** There is always somewhere cool for a dog to be.
- **Flat-faced, heavy-coated, overweight, elderly, and compromised dogs get different rules.** Bulldogs, Frenchies, pugs, boxers, and heavy northern breeds handle heat badly — shorter sessions, cooler hours, closer watching. Remind us at drop-off.

**What heatstroke looks like**, because every dog owner in this county should know it: frantic panting that doesn't slow with rest, bright red or very pale gums, thick ropey drool, stumbling, vomiting, collapse. Cool your dog with room-temperature water — not ice — and drive to a vet.

## SECTION 7 — STRUCTURE AND CONTAINMENT

**H2**
## Structure and containment

**Structure lowers stress.** Dogs settle faster where things happen in the same order every day. That predictability is why most dogs stop looking for the door around day two or three.

**Introductions are staged.** No dog is dropped into a group to see how it goes. Every new dog is assessed, then introduced deliberately, and dogs who shouldn't be in a group aren't put in one.

**Leashes on near vehicles and gates, every time.** The most common way a dog gets loose at any facility isn't a fence failure — it's a car door, a gate held a second too long, or a dog backing out of a collar. Those are procedure problems, so we run procedures.

## SECTION 10 — SAFETY FAQ

**H2**
## Safety and care FAQ

**`FAQPage` schema. Must match rendered text exactly.**
**⚠️ Do not add questions covering the four unconfirmed items in §9.**

**What vaccinations does my dog need at Floppy Ears Ranch?**
Current rabies, DHPP, and Bordetella are required for every dog on the property, whether they're boarding, in daycare, or in a training program. Canine influenza is recommended but not required. Bring records at drop-off or have your vet email them ahead — no current records, no admission.

**Do you feed my dog's own food, and can you give medication?**
Yes to both, at no extra charge. Send the food your dog normally eats, in the amount they normally eat, for the length of the stay plus a couple of extra days — changing a dog's diet at the same moment you change their whole environment usually produces several days of GI upset. Send medication in its original labeled container with written instructions covering dose, schedule, and whether it's given with food. We won't start, stop, or adjust anything on our own, and you'll hear from us if a dose is refused or vomited.

**Is it safe to board a dog here in the summer?**
Yes, and it's the thing we plan hardest around, because Corona runs triple digits for long stretches. Training and hard play run at the cooler ends of the day, midday is rest and shade, and water is everywhere and refreshed continuously. The arena turf is cooled and the indoor kennels are climate-controlled. We check ground temperature by hand before dogs work on a surface. Flat-faced breeds, heavy-coated breeds, and overweight, elderly, or compromised dogs get a shorter, cooler, more closely supervised day.

**What happens if my dog gets sick while they're with you?**
You hear from us the same day, by phone. A dog showing signs of anything contagious comes out of the group immediately and stays out until it's resolved. Kennels are cleaned daily and sanitized between dogs to keep it from spreading in the first place, and we don't save health news for pickup.

**Do I need to bring bedding, bowls, or toys?**
No. We supply bedding, bowls, leashes, collars, and all training equipment. Send food, medications, and vaccination records. Leave toys and blankets at home.
