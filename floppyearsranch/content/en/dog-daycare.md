## SECTION 2 — KENNEL-FREE DAYCARE IN THE JAX WELLER ARENA

**H2**
## Kennel-free daycare in the Jax Weller Arena

Daycare here doesn't happen in a kennel. It happens in the Jax Weller Arena — cooled turf, shade structures, multiple pools, elevated beds, and dog-safe sanitization.

Kennel-free means what it says. Your dog isn't rotated between a crate and a yard on a timer. They're out in the space, with the group and with people, for the day. Elevated beds and shade are there for dogs who want to check out for a while, and most use them at some point — but that's a dog choosing to rest, not a dog being put away.

The cooled turf matters more than it sounds like it should. Corona runs triple digits for stretches of the summer, and standard turf gets hot enough to hurt a dog's pads well before the air temperature looks alarming.

The arena is named for Jax, the shelter dog Kyla adopted in 2012. His behavior problems are the reason she went into dog training, which is the reason this business exists. The arena was built as his memorial.

## SECTION 3 — WHAT A DAY LOOKS LIKE

**H2**
## What a day looks like

**Drop-off, 8:00am to 8:00pm.** Pull in, hand off the leash, go to work. No appointment window to hit.

**Settling in.** Your dog joins the group, or a smaller group, depending on who's here that day. New and quieter dogs start on the edges and work in.

**Play, in cycles.** Groups run in stretches with real breaks between them, because a dog who plays hard for nine straight hours isn't having a great day — they're going home over-aroused and difficult. Play, break, water, shade, play again.

**Water and shade all day.** Pools in the summer. Hard play moves to the cooler ends of the day when it's hot, and that isn't a request line.

**Rest.** Elevated beds and quiet time in the middle of the day. Every dog takes it whether they think they want it or not.

**Pickup, up to 12 hours, through 8:00pm.** Your dog comes home tired in the good way — settled, not fried.

## SECTION 4 — WHY TRAINER-RUN DAYCARE IS DIFFERENT

**H2**
## Why trainer-run daycare is different

The people watching your dog play read dog body language for a living. That's the difference, and it changes two things.

**Problems get caught early.** A dog stiffening up, a play session tipping from fun into pressure, one dog getting cornered, a dog guarding a pool — those get interrupted at the body-language stage, before anything happens. Most daycare incidents aren't sudden. They're a slow build nobody with the right eyes was watching.

**Your dog doesn't come home worse.** Dogs practice whatever they do all day. A daycare where dogs blow past every boundary for ten hours produces dogs who blow past boundaries at home too. We run structure through the day — waiting at doors, calm before going out, coming when called, settling on cue. Not a training session. Just a day that reinforces the rules you're enforcing at home.

If you're doing training with us or anywhere else, daycare here supports it instead of quietly undoing it.

## SECTION 5 — THE ASSESSMENT

**H2**
## The assessment

Every new dog gets assessed before their first full day. It's short, and it's mostly us watching.

We meet your dog, see how they handle a new place and new people, introduce a couple of appropriate dogs, and watch. What we're looking for: play style, arousal level, how they handle being told to stop, whether they can settle, and how they respond to a dog who tells them off.

You get one of three answers. Yes, group daycare is a good fit. Yes, but in a smaller group. Or no — and if it's no, we'll tell you what we saw and what would actually help.

We don't run everybody through the same yard and sort it out later. That's how facilities end up making phone calls nobody wants to make.

## SECTION 6 — WHO DAYCARE IS RIGHT FOR — AND WHO IT ISN'T

**H2**
## Who daycare is right for — and who it isn't

### H3 — Daycare usually works well for

- Social dogs who genuinely enjoy other dogs
- High-energy adolescents and young adults who are climbing the walls at home
- Dogs left alone all day while you work
- Dogs who need to burn energy so the evening isn't a fight
- Dogs already comfortable in groups from a previous facility or a training program

### H3 — Daycare is usually the wrong call for

- Dogs who are reactive or aggressive toward other dogs
- Dogs who are genuinely fearful in groups — daycare doesn't fix that, it rehearses it
- Dogs recovering from surgery or on activity restrictions
- Puppies who haven't finished enough of their vaccine series
- Dogs who are selective, older, or simply don't enjoy other dogs

That last group is worth saying plainly: a dog who doesn't like other dogs is not a broken dog. Plenty of normal dogs would rather not spend nine hours with strangers, and forcing it doesn't make them social, it makes them worse.

If that's your dog, daycare isn't your solution. Overnight boarding with solo turnout, or a training program that addresses the actual issue, will do more for you.

## SECTION 8 — VACCINATION REQUIREMENTS

**H2**
## Vaccination requirements

Every dog in daycare needs current **rabies**, **DHPP**, and **Bordetella**. **Canine influenza** is recommended but not required.

Bring records to the assessment or have your vet email them ahead. Bordetella is the one that lapses most often — most vets want it every six to twelve months for dogs in group settings, so check the date rather than assuming.

Puppies can start once they've had enough of their vaccine series to be safe around other dogs. Call us and we'll walk through the timing rather than guessing at it here.

## SECTION 10 — DAYCARE FAQ

**H2**
## Dog daycare FAQ

**`FAQPage` schema. Must match rendered text exactly.**

**How much does dog daycare cost in Corona, CA?**
Daycare at Floppy Ears Ranch is $40 for a full day of up to 12 hours. A 5-day package is $180, which works out to $36 a day, and a 10-day package is $340, which works out to $34 a day. There's no surcharge for a long day inside our 8:00am to 8:00pm window.

**What does kennel-free daycare mean?**
It means your dog spends the day in the Jax Weller Arena with the group and with people, not rotated in and out of a crate on a schedule. The arena has cooled turf, shade structures, multiple pools, and elevated beds. Dogs rest when they want to rest, but resting is a choice they make, not a place we put them.

**Does my dog need an assessment first?**
Yes. Every new dog is assessed before their first full day. We watch how your dog handles a new environment, new people, and a few appropriate dogs, then tell you whether group daycare is a fit, whether a smaller group would be better, or whether daycare isn't right for your dog at all.

**Do you take reactive or aggressive dogs in daycare?**
No. Group daycare is the wrong environment for a dog who's reactive or aggressive toward other dogs — it rehearses the problem instead of solving it. We do assess reactive and aggressive dogs individually for overnight boarding with solo turnout, and we run training programs that address the behavior itself.

**What vaccinations does my dog need for daycare?**
Current rabies, DHPP, and Bordetella are required. Canine influenza is recommended but not required. Bring records to the assessment.

**How long can my dog stay?**
Up to 12 hours on a full day. Drop-off and pickup run 8:00am to 8:00pm, seven days a week.

**Will daycare tire my dog out?**
Yes, but not by running them for twelve straight hours. Play runs in cycles with real rest built in, because a dog who's been over-aroused all day comes home wired rather than settled. The goal is a dog who sleeps well that night, not a dog who's wrecked.

**Can daycare help with my dog's behavior?**
Indirectly. Daycare is exercise and social time, not training — but because the people running it are trainers, the day is structured so it reinforces the rules you're working on at home instead of undoing them. If the behavior is the actual problem, a training program will do far more.
