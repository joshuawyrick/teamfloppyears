## SECTION 2 — WHAT OFF-LEASH RELIABILITY ACTUALLY MEANS

**H2**
## What off-leash reliability actually means

It means your dog does what you say when nothing is physically stopping them from doing otherwise.

That's a much higher bar than "my dog knows come." Most dogs know come. Most dogs also know it's negotiable when there's a rabbit involved, and the leash has been what settles that argument.

- **Recall that holds through the distraction**, not after it. A dog who comes back once the other dog has left isn't recalling, they're finishing.
- **Distance obedience.** Down and wait at forty yards.
- **An emergency stop.** Your dog freezes on command. That's the one that matters when a car is coming.
- **Work in real places.** Trails, fields, open ground — not just the yard where it was taught.
- **No management on your end.** No treat bag, no repeating yourself, no bargaining.

**What it does not mean:** a dog who can be trusted anywhere, under any circumstance, forever, with no maintenance. Nobody honest sells that. What we build is a dog reliable enough that off-leash freedom is a reasonable decision in the right places — and an owner who can tell a right place from a wrong one.

## SECTION 5 — HOW WE USE AN E-COLLAR

**H2**
## How we use an e-collar — honestly

We use an e-collar in this program. The equipment is included in the $4,500 and you're taught to use it before your dog goes home. Here's what that actually is, without the euphemisms.

**An e-collar is a remote communication tool.** A modern collar has a wide range of stimulation levels plus vibration and tone. At training levels, most dogs respond to something a person holding the contacts in their own hand would call a tap.

**We don't start with the collar.** Your dog learns every command on leash first, with markers and rewards, until they know what the word means. The collar goes on later, unpaired and unused, so wearing it becomes normal before it means anything.

**It closes the gap between "knows" and "does."** Once a dog understands a command, the collar lets us say "I mean it" at a hundred feet, where a leash can't reach and shouting doesn't work. Turning the sensation off the instant the dog complies is what teaches the behavior — the dog learns they control it, which is why a well-trained dog on an e-collar looks confident rather than nervous.

**It is not a punishment button.** Correcting a dog for failing something they don't understand yet is how e-collars earned their reputation. It's also useless. You can't punish a dog into knowledge.

**We teach you.** Level-finding, fit, rotating position, timing. You leave able to run the tool, not dependent on us.

**And we tell you before you book.** Not on day four, not in a contract clause. On the consultation call, with the chance to say no thanks.

## SECTION 6 — IS E-COLLAR TRAINING SAFE?

**H2**
## Is e-collar training safe?

Yes, when the collar is fitted correctly, used at the lowest level your dog responds to, and used to communicate about behavior the dog already understands. The risks come from misuse — a collar strapped on too tight for too long, a level set far higher than needed, or corrections applied to a dog who was never taught the command. Those are handling failures, not properties of the equipment.

**On the sensation.** A modern e-collar produces muscle stimulation, the same category as a TENS unit at a physical therapist's office. It doesn't burn. We find your dog's working level by starting at zero and moving up to the first flicker of recognition — an ear twitch, a head turn. Usually far lower than owners expect.

**On the marks people worry about.** Pressure sores come from a collar left tight, in one position, for hours. Prevention is boring and it works: correct fit, rotate position, take it off daily, check the skin.

**On stress and fallout.** This is the real debate and it deserves better than a slogan. Poor timing, an unfairly high level, or corrections aimed at a confused dog can absolutely produce a stressed, shut-down dog. That's why the sequence matters: teach on leash, confirm understanding, introduce the collar unpaired, then use it at the lowest effective level on something already known.

**On what it doesn't fix.** An e-collar is not a treatment for fear, anxiety, or aggression, and using it as one tends to make those worse.

**Where we stand.** We're a balanced facility: relationship and marker training first, then clear corrections so a dog understands both sides of the conversation. We won't pretend to be in a different camp to make a sale. If e-collar training isn't something you want, say so — we'll tell you honestly what's achievable without it, and sometimes the answer is "quite a lot, but not full off-leash freedom in an open field."

## SECTION 7 — CALIFORNIA LEASH LAWS

**H2**
## California leash laws — what off-leash does and doesn't mean

Training your dog to be reliable off leash does not make it legal to have them off leash. Those are separate questions, and a well-trained dog running loose in the wrong place is still a citation and still your liability.

**There's no single statewide leash law covering every public place in California.** Requirements are set locally — city ordinance, county ordinance, and the rules of whatever park, beach, trail, or agency land you're standing on. The rule can change when you cross a street.

**In practice, around here:** most cities in Riverside County, including Corona, require dogs to be under leash control in public. California State Parks and most state beaches require leashes and keep dogs off many trails entirely. Regional parks set their own rules. Designated off-leash dog parks are the exception.

**The practical version:** check the rule for the specific place first. Posted signage is the fastest answer. "But my dog is trained" is not a defense anyone is required to accept.

**On liability, which matters more than the fine.** California holds dog owners strictly liable for bites in most circumstances — it generally doesn't matter whether your dog had shown a problem before. Off leash in public, you carry that exposure with no way to intervene physically. That's an argument for being honest about where your dog has actually earned it, not against the training.

**Where the training genuinely pays off:** your own property, private land you have permission to use, designated off-leash areas, open space where dogs are permitted off leash, dog sports — and every on-leash situation, because a dog who recalls off a rabbit at fifty yards doesn't drag you down a sidewalk.

**We're trainers, not lawyers.** Rules vary and change. Verify the current ordinance wherever you're going.
