## SECTION 2 — IT STARTED WITH A DOG NOBODY WANTED

**H2**
## It started with a dog nobody wanted

In 2012, Kyla was still in school when she adopted a dog named Jax from a shelter in Central California.

Jax had problems. Not the cute kind. The kind that make people quietly return a dog after two weeks and tell themselves it wasn't the right time. Kyla didn't return him. She started reading, then asking, then paying people who knew more than she did, and by 2013 she was interning under a professional trainer and building a career she had not planned on having.

Jax got better. Then he got good. He earned his AKC Canine Good Citizen certification. He worked as a service medical alert dog. He spent years as a training assistant here, the steady dog you put in front of a reactive one to show them that nothing bad happens.

He died in April. The Jax Weller Arena — the kennel-free space where daycare runs — was built as his memorial, and it's the part of this property we're proudest of.

## How the ranch came together

Chad came to this from somewhere else entirely. He was drafted into a St. Louis Cardinals affiliate in 2004 and spent four years in the organization, then played six more seasons professionally in Brisbane, Australia. He moved from Dallas to Norwalk, California in 2015.

He learned dog training from Kyla. Then he went after it the way you go after something that has taken hold of you — seminars, mentorships, other people's facilities, thousands of repetitions. He's handled more than a thousand dogs now. Obedience and fear-and-aggression rehabilitation are where he does his best work, and his own dog Rosie was a rehab case before she was a family dog.

Chad and Kyla married in 2018. Their daughter Peyton was born in 2017. The company was founded in 2019.

That order matters, because it explains the place. This isn't a franchise somebody bought into. It's a family that built a business around the thing they were already doing at home, on land they could work dogs on, and then kept it small enough to still do the work themselves.

Three people work here. When you call, you get one of them.

## SECTION 4 — THE PIT BULL WORK

**H2**
## The pit bull work

We've helped rescue more than 200 pit bulls.

That number belongs on this page for a reason, and it isn't marketing. It's the clearest statement of what this business believes: that the dogs written off fastest are usually the ones a person never bothered to train, and that the label on a dog tells you almost nothing about the dog.

Pit bulls fill the shelters in this county. They sit there longest, get adopted last, and come back most often — usually for behavior a competent trainer could have addressed in the first month. Some of that is real. Most of it is a stigma doing its work.

We take those cases. We work with rescue organizations. We've taken dogs into this facility that had run out of options somewhere else, and we've handed a fair number of them back to families who now describe them as the easiest dog they've ever owned.

We won't pretend every one of those stories ended that way. Some dogs don't come around. Some do, and the home falls apart anyway. But 200 is 200, and we intend to keep adding to it.

**If you work with a rescue in Riverside County or the Inland Empire, call us.** We'd rather be part of that pipeline than wait for the dogs to reach us after the third adoption fails.

## SECTION 5 — WHAT WE BELIEVE

**H2**
## What we believe

Five things, and we'll hold ourselves to them.

**Tell people the truth, including the parts that cost us money.** If your dog needs private lessons instead of a $2,900 program, we'll say that on the phone. If we don't think we can help, we'll say that too. A client who books the wrong thing is a bad outcome for both of us and it always comes back around.

**The dog isn't the problem you think it is.** Almost every dog we meet is doing something that made complete sense from where they were standing. Our job is to change what makes sense to them, not to punish them for arriving at the wrong answer with the information they had.

**Training that doesn't transfer to you isn't training.** A dog who works beautifully for a trainer and falls apart at home cost you money for nothing. That's why every program ends with a session where the leash goes in your hand and you practice until it works for you, not until we've demonstrated it.

**Say which tools you use, in writing, before anyone pays.** We're a balanced training facility. We build on relationship and marker training, and we use corrections, including an e-collar in the off-leash program. You will never find out mid-program that we're using equipment you weren't expecting.

**No dog gets a guarantee.** Anybody promising you a guaranteed outcome on aggression is either inexperienced or lying. What we guarantee is support — free refresher lessons and answers to your questions for as long as you own the dog, with no expiration and no upsell attached.

## SECTION 8 — WHO WE'RE NOT FOR

**H2**
## Who we're probably not right for

We'd rather say this here than on a phone call after you've gotten your hopes up.

- **If you want purely positive, no-corrections training,** we're not your facility. That's a legitimate approach and there are good trainers in this area who do it well. We're not one of them, and we'd rather you find them than be unhappy with us.
- **If you want the dog fixed without changing anything at home,** it won't hold. Every program here requires you to participate at the end. That part is not optional and it's not upsold.
- **If you need daycare or regular boarding and you live in Irvine,** the drive doesn't work. Training is a drop-off you make twice. Daycare is a commute you make daily.
- **If you want a guarantee on an aggression case,** nobody honest can give you one.
