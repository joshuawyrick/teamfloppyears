## SECTION 2 — WHO IT'S FOR

**H2**
## Who private training is best for

Board & Train gets more attention on this site because it's the bigger program. It isn't automatically the better one.

**Private lessons are the right call when:**

- You want to learn to train dogs, not just have a trained dog. Some people are going to own dogs for the next forty years and would rather own the skill.
- The problem is narrow. One command, one situation, one habit.
- You have the time. Fifteen focused minutes a day beats a two-hour session once a week, and if you'll actually do the fifteen minutes, lessons work.
- Your dog is very fearful and moving into a new place would set them back before it helped.
- Budget is the deciding factor. Five sessions is $900 against $2,900 for a program.
- The issue is really a household issue — four people handling one dog four different ways. That gets fixed by coaching the people.

**Private lessons are the wrong call when:**

- You're honestly not going to practice. This is the one that sinks people, and it's worth being honest with yourself about it before you spend $900.
- The behavior is escalating and waiting is unsafe.
- Your schedule is genuinely full for the next two months.
- You want off-leash reliability, which needs far more proofing than most owners can produce at home.

We'll tell you which one we think you need on the consultation. Recommending the cheaper option is a normal outcome of that call, not a rare one.

Sit, down, stay, place, heel, come when called, leave it, and door manners. Taught to you first, then to your dog, because the timing is the hard part and the timing is yours.

### H3 — Behavior problems
Jumping, counter surfing, door dashing, barking, stealing, digging, not settling, guarding the couch, ignoring you entirely. Ordinary problems, and most of them come apart quickly once the structure is consistent.

### H3 — Fear and anxiety
Fearful dogs often do better in lessons than in a program, because the work depends on trust and the trust is with you. Slower, quieter sessions. Separation anxiety in particular is largely homework — it happens when you're not there, so it has to be built where it happens.

### H3 — Leash work
Loose-leash walking, a real heel, and the early stages of reactivity work. This is the single most requested thing we teach in lessons, and it's the one that changes daily life the most.

## SECTION 4 — WHAT A LESSON LOOKS LIKE

**H2**
## What happens during a lesson

**You do most of the work.** We'll demonstrate a mechanic on your dog so you can see what it's supposed to look like, then hand the leash straight back. A trainer who spends the hour showing you how well your dog listens to them has taught you nothing.

**We watch you, not just the dog.** Most of what goes wrong in home training is timing, leash pressure, body position, and repeating a command four times. You can't see yourself doing any of that. We can.

**One or two things per session.** Not eight. A lesson that dumps a curriculum on you produces a week where nothing gets practiced properly.

**You leave with homework.** Specific, short, and daily. What to practice, how many reps, where, and what it should look like when it's working.

**Next session starts with what happened.** Including the parts that went badly, which are the useful parts. Tell us it fell apart on Thursday — that's the information we need.

## SECTION 5 — FEARFUL AND AGGRESSIVE DOGS

**H2**
## Private training for fearful or aggressive dogs

For some dogs, lessons are the better starting point specifically because they're the slower one.

A deeply fearful dog needs to build trust before anything else can happen, and moving into an unfamiliar facility can cost weeks of progress before it buys any. A dog whose aggression is tied to the home, the family, or one specific relationship in the house has a problem that partly has to be worked where it lives. And some owners need to develop handling confidence before they can hold up their end of a program — that's not a criticism, it's just true, and it's fixable.

A common path we recommend: start with a few private lessons, get the trust and the handling in place, then decide whether a program makes sense. Nobody is going to push you toward the $3,500 option on the first call.

Aggression cases always start with a behavior evaluation, whichever direction they go.
