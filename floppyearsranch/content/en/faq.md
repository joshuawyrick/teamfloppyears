## SECTION 2 — GETTING STARTED

**H2**
## Getting started

**`FAQPage` schema. Must match rendered text exactly.**

**What's the very first thing I should do?**
Call [PHONE], text us, or fill out the intake form. The first step is a free 30-minute phone or video consultation where you tell us what's happening and we tell you what we'd do about it. Nothing is booked and nothing is charged on that call.

**How quickly will someone get back to me?**
We aim to respond to every inquiry within one business day, usually the same day. If you've got a dog with a bite history, say so in the form — those go to a priority queue rather than the ordinary one.

**Do I have to bring my dog to the first appointment?**
Not to the phone consultation. The second step, the $50 in-person facility evaluation, is where your dog comes out to the ranch. That $50 comes off whatever program you book, so if you book, the evaluation effectively costs nothing.

**What happens at the in-person evaluation?**
It runs about an hour. We assess your dog's behavior and temperament, watch how they handle a new environment and new people, and talk through history — what's happened, what you've tried, what changed and when. You walk the property, meet everyone, and leave with a specific program recommendation and a price. Not a range. A price. If you'd rather just look at the place first, tours are free, take about twenty minutes, and don't require you to be considering anything.

**Do you have a waitlist?**
Programs book out, especially in summer and around the holidays. A deposit holds specific dates — $500 for Board & Train and Puppy Board & Train, $750 for the Behavior Rehabilitation and Off-Leash programs.

## SECTION 3 — CHOOSING A PROGRAM

**H2**
## Choosing a program

**Which program does my dog need?**
That's what the free consultation is for, and it's a genuine question rather than a sales step. Broadly: Board & Train if the problem is significant and your schedule won't support daily practice for the next two months, private lessons if you want to do the training yourself and have the time, the Behavior Rehabilitation Program if there's aggression or a bite history, and the Off-Leash Reliability Program if your dog already has a foundation and you want real freedom. We'll tell you which one we think fits — including when the answer is the cheaper one.

**Can you tell me over the phone whether you can help?**
Usually, roughly. We can tell you whether your situation is the kind of thing we work on and what we'd likely recommend. What we can't do on a phone call is assess a dog we've never seen, which is why aggression and bite-history cases always get an in-person evaluation before we commit to anything.

**Will you turn down a dog?**
Yes, and we do. Sometimes because a case needs veterinary behavior medicine rather than training. Sometimes because we don't believe we can make the dog safe in the home they're going back to. Sometimes because the owner wants a guarantee nobody honest can give. We'd rather say no on a free call than take $3,500 and disappoint you.

**My dog isn't bad, just untrained. Is that too small a problem for you?**
No. Most of the dogs here are ordinary family dogs who pull, jump, ignore recall, and can't settle. That's the bulk of the work and it's the most satisfying kind, because it changes daily life for a whole household.

## SECTION 5 — BOARD & TRAIN QUESTIONS

**H2**
## Board & Train

**Is three weeks really enough time?**
For most obedience and manners work, yes — because it's daily, concentrated, and in an environment we control. Three weeks here is more structured repetition than most dogs get in a year of weekly lessons. What three weeks can't do is finish a serious behavior case, which is why aggression work is its own program and why lifetime support exists.

**What if my dog needs more time than the program length?**
We'll tell you that during the program rather than at pickup, and we'll be honest about whether extending would genuinely help or just cost you money. Some dogs benefit. Some have hit what the environment can give them and need the work to move home.

**Can I call and check on my dog?**
Yes, any time. You'll already be getting a photo every day and video every three days, but nobody here treats a worried owner as a nuisance. Mid-program in-person visits are a different question — for most dogs they reset the adjustment, and we'll talk it through case by case.

**What does my dog do the rest of the day when they're not training?**
Rest, decompression, supervised turnout, and structured downtime. Two focused training sessions a day plus real-world work is the ceiling for genuine learning. A facility running dogs from open to close is producing tired dogs, not trained ones.

**Do you train more than one dog at a time?**
Yes, but every dog gets individual sessions. Group work happens on purpose, as proofing around distraction, not as a way to handle volume. And a lot of alumni board with us afterward — the dog already knows the place and the people, which makes it a much easier stay than a first-time boarding.

## SECTION 6 — BOARDING AND DAYCARE

**H2**
## Boarding and daycare

**Do I need a training program to board or use daycare?**
No. Boarding and daycare are open to any dog who passes the assessment, whether they've trained with us or not.

**Does my dog have to be social with other dogs?**
For daycare, yes — that's the entire premise. For boarding, no. Boarding dogs can be turned out solo, and we assess reactive and aggressive dogs individually for exactly that reason. A dog who doesn't like other dogs is not a broken dog, and plenty of perfectly normal dogs would rather not spend nine hours with strangers.

**How far ahead should I book for a holiday?**
Further ahead than feels necessary. Thanksgiving week and December 20 through January 2 fill first, then July 4th and Memorial Day weekend. People book those months out.

**Can you board two dogs from the same house together?**
Yes, if they genuinely get along unsupervised, and there's a $10 per night discount for each additional dog from the same household. If they scuffle over food or space at home, tell us and we'll house them separately — that's normal and it isn't a failure.

**Will boarding undo my dog's training?**
It shouldn't. Everyone here is a trainer, and the day is structured around the same rules — waiting at doors, settling on cue, coming when called. That's the practical difference between a trainer-run facility and a kennel with staff.

## SECTION 7 — BEHAVIOR PROBLEMS

**H2**
## Behavior problems

**My dog is fine with me and terrible with everyone else. Is that fixable?**
Usually, and it's one of the most common things we see. A dog who's comfortable with their own people and defensive with everyone else is generally not "protective" — they're uncertain, and they've learned that making a scene works. That's addressable, though how long it takes depends on how long they've been rehearsing it.

**Three trainers have already told me my dog is a lost cause. Should I bother calling?**
Call. That's a meaningful share of the dogs that come here, and this business exists because Kyla didn't return a shelter dog everyone would have understood her returning. We can't promise an outcome, and we'll be straight with you after we've seen your dog. But we'll look.

**Is my dog's behavior my fault?**
Almost never in the way people mean when they ask it. Genetics, early experience, health, and rehearsal matter more than anything you did or didn't do. What's in your hands is what happens next.

**Could this be medical?**
It's worth ruling out, especially if the change was sudden. Pain, thyroid problems, and vision or hearing loss produce behavior that looks exactly like aggression, anxiety, or stubbornness. We'd rather you spend money at the vet first than spend it here on a training problem that isn't one.

**Will getting a second dog help?**
Usually not, and it frequently makes things worse. A second dog doesn't teach the first one manners — it gives them company while they practice the same behavior, and now you have two dogs doing it. Solve the first dog first.

## SECTION 8 — OUR METHODS

**H2**
## Our methods

**Are you a balanced trainer or a positive-only trainer?**
Balanced. We teach with markers, food, play, and relationship first, then add clear corrections once the dog knows the behavior. We use a prong collar or an e-collar on some dogs when the situation calls for it. We say this up front on purpose — finding out mid-program is how people end up angry, and it's a problem the trainer created.

**Can I ask you not to use a particular tool on my dog?**
Yes, and you should if you feel strongly. We'll tell you honestly what we think that means for your goal — for some goals it changes very little, and for reliable off-leash freedom it changes a lot. Either way it's your dog and your call, made before you book rather than discovered after.

**Do I have to keep using the tools at home?**
Depends on the tool and the dog. Most dogs are faded off a prong collar. An e-collar is more often kept as a safety net specifically for off-leash access. Either way you'll be taught to use whatever your dog goes home on, properly and under supervision, before they leave.

**Do you guarantee results?**
No, and be careful with anyone who does — particularly on aggression. What we guarantee is support: free refresher lessons and answers to your questions for as long as you own the dog, with no expiration and nothing upsold on the back of it.
