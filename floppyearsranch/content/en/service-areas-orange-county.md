## SECTION 2 — THE DRIVE

**H2**
## You make this drive twice. Not twice a week.

Start with the map, because that's where the objection lives.

Orange County people hear "Corona" and picture the 91 at 5:30 on a Thursday, which is a fair thing to picture. It's one of the worst commutes in Southern California and we won't pretend otherwise. If we were asking you out every Saturday for eight weeks of lessons, you should say no.

We're not. Board & Train is a drop-off. Your dog lives here for three weeks — six if it's a puppy — and you make the drive exactly twice: once to bring your dog, once for the transfer session where we hand the training back to you.

Two round trips. That's the entire ask. And on a mid-morning weekday or a Sunday, the run through the Santa Ana Canyon from Anaheim Hills to the Corona line is short. The 91 Express Lanes are there for the days it isn't. Come at 10am instead of 5pm and this stops being a conversation.

**What you get for it:** five acres. Open ground to proof recall on, indoor climate-controlled kennels, and room to work a reactive dog at a distance from its trigger instead of six feet from it in a parking lot. There isn't much five-acre training land left in Orange County. That's the trade — two drives for space.

## SECTION 4 — CITY BY CITY

**H2**
## Drive times from Orange County

### H3 — Yorba Linda — about 25 minutes
The shortest drive in the county. Out Imperial Highway to the 91 and east through the canyon, and you're here. Yorba Linda has large lots, an extensive equestrian trail network, and a lot of dogs living with more room than structure — which produces exactly the recall problem the Off-Leash Reliability Program is built for.

### H3 — Anaheim Hills — about 25 minutes
The first neighborhood you hit on the OC side of the canyon, and the easiest drive on this page. Hillside streets, canyon edges, and homes backing onto open space where coyotes are a routine fact of life. A single bad coyote encounter is one of the more common origins of the reactivity cases we take from this area.

### H3 — Brea — about 30 minutes
Either the 57 to the 91, or Carbon Canyon Road east through Chino Hills to the 71 and down — the back way, and often the calmer one. Brea's mix of older neighborhoods and newer hillside tracts sends us both first-family puppies and long-standing leash reactivity.

### H3 — Fullerton — 30 to 35 minutes
The 91 east from the 57. Fullerton is a walking city with the Fullerton Loop, Laguna Lake, and a dense downtown, which means dogs here are out in public constantly. Excellent for a dog with manners. Rough for a dog without them, and that's usually why people call.

### H3 — Placentia — about 30 minutes
Straight shot down Orangethorpe or Yorba Linda Boulevard to the 91. Quiet, established neighborhoods, older housing stock, and a lot of family dogs whose obedience never quite got finished.

### H3 — Orange — 30 to 35 minutes
The 55 north to the 91 east. Between Old Towne's dense grid and the trails at Santiago Oaks and Irvine Regional Park, dogs from Orange live a public life. Off-leash reliability and solid loose-leash walking do most of the work here.

### H3 — Irvine — 40 to 50 minutes
The 261 or 133 to the 241, then the 91 east — the toll roads make this considerably better than the map suggests. Irvine is master-planned, apartment-heavy, and rule-heavy, which produces a specific problem: a dog with a barking or reactivity issue in Irvine isn't just a household stressor, it's a letter from the HOA. Board & Train exists partly for people on a deadline they didn't set.

### H3 — Tustin — 40 to 45 minutes
The 55 north to the 91. Old Town Tustin's walkable streets and the trails at Peters Canyon put a lot of dogs in front of a lot of other dogs, daily.

### H3 — Costa Mesa — 45 to 50 minutes
The 55 all the way north, then east on the 91. Dense, walkable, dog-saturated — leash reactivity country.

### H3 — Newport Beach — 45 to 55 minutes
The 73 to the 55, then the 91. Back Bay loop dogs, Buck Gully dogs, and a lot of owners who want off-leash reliability specifically because so much of what they'd like to do with their dog requires it.

### H3 — Huntington Beach — 50 to 55 minutes
The 405 to the 55 or the 22 to the 91, depending on the hour. Worth being blunt about: Dog Beach is the reason a lot of HB owners eventually call a trainer. An off-leash beach is a wonderful thing with a dog that recalls under distraction and a genuinely bad afternoon with one that doesn't.

### H3 — Mission Viejo — about an hour, give or take
The farthest we go in the county, and it works because of the 241. If you're in south OC, be honest with yourself about whether you'll make the second trip for the transfer session — because that session is the part that makes the rest of it stick. If the answer is yes, the distance is fine.

## SECTION 5 — BEHAVIOR WORK

**H2**
## Why behavior cases travel

Aggression and severe reactivity are the two problems people will drive an hour for, and there's a reason.

An ordinary obedience class can't take your dog. A daycare won't. Trainers who work these cases seriously are thin on the ground anywhere, and the work needs space — room to put a dog at a distance from a trigger where it can actually think, then close that distance in increments over weeks. That's hard to do in a room.

The Behavior Rehabilitation Program is three weeks at $3,500, for aggression, bite histories, and reactivity severe enough that ordinary obedience isn't the answer. Every dog is evaluated individually before we accept the case, because what's driving the behavior changes what we do about it — and because not every dog is a candidate. We don't guarantee outcomes on aggression. Anyone who does is selling you something.

If we don't think we can help your dog safely, we'll tell you on the phone and it will cost you nothing.

## SECTION 6 — HOW IT WORKS FROM OUT OF AREA

**H2**
## How this works if you're an hour away

**1. Free 30-minute phone or video consultation.** No drive. We ask about the history, what you've tried, and what you want, then tell you whether we think we can help.

**2. The evaluation.** $50 in person, credited in full toward your program. If the drive is the obstacle, ask about the $139 virtual consultation — an hour, any behavior, anywhere. Not a substitute for hands on the dog, but a real assessment.

**3. Drop-off.** Food for the stay, medications, vaccination records. We supply everything else. Come mid-morning and skip the canyon.

**4. Three weeks, or six.** Photo every day, video every third day, a call partway through, and you can text us whenever.

**5. The transfer session.** This is the trip that matters. Block out real time — we put the leash in your hand and coach you until the handling works for you, not until we've demonstrated it.

**6. Afterward.** Free refresher lessons and lifetime support, no expiration. Distance doesn't change that.

## Orange County dog training FAQ

**Do you offer Board & Train for Orange County?**
Yes. Board & Train is three weeks at $2,900 for dogs six months and older, six weeks at $3,200 for puppies, $3,500 for the three-week Behavior Rehabilitation Program, and $4,500 for the Off-Leash Reliability Program. We're 25 to 40 minutes from north Orange County and 40 to 55 from Irvine, Tustin, and Newport.

**Do you offer dog daycare or boarding in Orange County?**
No. Daycare and routine overnight boarding are only offered to dogs within about fifteen minutes of our Corona facility. Driving from Orange County twice a day isn't realistic, and claiming otherwise would be dishonest. What we do offer Orange County is Board & Train and behavior rehabilitation, which are drop-off programs.

**Is the 91 going to make this miserable?**
At 5pm on a weekday, yes. At 10am, on a weekend, or in the reverse-commute direction, the canyon run between Anaheim Hills and Corona is short. You're making the trip twice for a three-week program, so schedule both trips off-peak and the drive stops being a factor. The 91 Express Lanes are there for the days it doesn't cooperate.

**Why would I drive to Corona when there are trainers in Orange County?**
Space, mostly. We have five acres — open ground for proofing recall, room to work a reactive dog at a distance from its trigger, and indoor climate-controlled kennels rather than a boarding arrangement bolted onto a retail suite. If a trainer near you has the room and the approach you want, take it. We'd rather you get a good outcome close to home than a mediocre one an hour away.

**Can I visit my dog during the program if I'm coming from Irvine?**
We'll talk about it on the consultation, but for most dogs mid-program visits reset progress rather than help. That's true regardless of distance — it just happens to also save you a drive. You get a photo every day, video every three days, a call partway through, and we answer texts.

**Do you take aggression cases from Orange County?**
Yes, and a large share of our out-of-area clients are behavior cases. Every dog is evaluated individually before we accept it, we don't promise outcomes, and if we don't think we can help yours safely we'll say so on the free phone call.

**What if I can't make the transfer session?**
Then tell us before you book, because that session is what makes the training last. We'd rather work out the scheduling in advance — or tell you honestly that a trainer closer to home is the better call — than hand your dog back at the curb and watch three weeks of work come apart.
