## SECTION 2 — THE EASTVALE SITUATION

**H2**
## A new city full of new dogs

Eastvale barely existed twenty-five years ago. It was dairy land, and then in about a decade and a half it became a city of tract neighborhoods, cul-de-sacs, three-car garages, and a high school that fields a team in everything. It incorporated in 2010. Most of the housing stock is newer than most of the dogs living in it.

That history shows up in the training work.

The typical Eastvale household is young, both adults work, there are kids, and the weekends are spoken for by travel ball and tournaments. The dog arrived after the house did — a doodle, a golden, a shepherd, a rescue somebody's cousin knew about — and it arrived into a schedule that was already full. Yards are tidy and small. Sidewalks are continuous and busy, which means your dog meets four other dogs on a fifteen-minute walk whether you wanted that or not.

None of this is a complaint about Eastvale. It's just a specific set of conditions, and it reliably produces a specific set of dogs: under-structured, over-stimulated, well-loved, and confused about who's in charge.

## SECTION 4 — THE FOUR-HANDLERS PROBLEM

**H2**
## When four people train the same dog four different ways

This is the single most common thing we see from family households, and it's worth naming plainly.

Dogs are not confused by rules. They're confused by rules that change depending on who's in the room. Dad says off the couch. The eleven-year-old says nothing. Mom says off but doesn't mean it. The six-year-old feeds the dog under the table because it's funny. Grandma is visiting and none of it applies.

From the dog's side, the couch rule is a coin flip. So the dog keeps flipping the coin, and everybody concludes the dog is stubborn.

Fixing that is less about the dog than about the household. Our transfer session — the part at the end where we hand the training back — is where this gets sorted, and for family dogs we want everybody there who's old enough to hold a leash. Kids included. A ten-year-old who knows how to ask for a place command correctly is worth more to your dog's training than another week of our time.

We also work the manners that actually matter in a house with children: no jumping on people, no bolting through the front door when the kids leave for school, settling on a cot while dinner happens, polite greetings when six of your daughter's teammates come through the door at once, and leaving food alone.

## SECTION 5 — ADOLESCENCE

**H2**
## Somewhere between six and twelve months, it falls apart

Nobody warns new owners about this, so we do.

Your well-behaved puppy will appear to forget everything it learned. Recall stops working. The leash manners evaporate. A dog that was fine with other dogs starts barking at them on the sidewalk. This is normal, it's developmental, and it is the exact point where most families quit and decide they have a bad dog.

It's also the point where a dense, walkable neighborhood works against you. Every trip down the street is another rep of your adolescent dog rehearsing whatever it's currently doing wrong, in front of an audience of other dogs doing the same thing.

If you're in it right now, the three-week Board & Train at $2,900 exists for this. If you're not there yet, know it's coming and build the foundation before it arrives.

## SECTION 9 — FAQ

**H2**
## Eastvale dog training FAQ

**`FAQPage` schema. Must match rendered text exactly.**

**Do you offer dog training in Eastvale, CA?**
Yes. Eastvale is roughly fifteen minutes from our five-acre facility in Corona, and every program we run is available — puppy training, Board & Train, private lessons, behavior work, boarding, and daycare.

**How old does my puppy need to be to start training?**
Puppies six months and under go into the six-week Puppy Board & Train at $3,200. A puppy can start once it's had enough of its vaccination series to be safe around other dogs — we'll walk through the timing on the free phone consultation, because it depends on where your puppy is in the schedule.

**Can my kids be part of the training?**
Yes, and we want them there for the transfer session if they're old enough to hold a leash. Most family-dog problems are consistency problems, and consistency includes the kids. A child who knows how to give a command correctly does more for your dog than an extra week of our time.

**My dog is great at home and terrible on the neighborhood walk. Why?**
Because home is easy and the sidewalk isn't. Eastvale streets are dense with dogs, kids, bikes, and strollers, and a dog that has never been proofed around distraction will fall apart the moment it meets one. We train the skills in a controlled setting and then deliberately make conditions harder until the behavior holds outside your house.

**Is Board & Train worth it if we could just take classes?**
Sometimes it isn't, and we'll say so. If you genuinely have the hours to practice daily for the next two months, private lessons at $200 a session will teach you more and cost you less. If the calendar is already full — work, school, tournaments — Board & Train exists because the repetitions have to happen somewhere.

**Do you take dogs from Eastvale for daycare?**
Yes. Fifteen minutes is close enough for daycare to be a routine rather than a project, and a lot of Eastvale dogs come one or two days a week because the house is empty all day. An assessment is required before the first full day.
